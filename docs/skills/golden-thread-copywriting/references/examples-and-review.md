# Original examples and review cases

These are teaching drafts, not approved or scheduled marketing sends. Examples contain no send permissions or invented offer terms.

## One idea, progressively made real

Thread: **Give time in nature a place in your year.**

| Job | Original draft | How it develops the idea |
|---|---|---|
| Recognise the desire | A year with room for the wilderness. | Gives the reader a desired picture |
| Make the place credible | Find a place to get to know. | Introduces actual landscapes and care for the land |
| Explain the mechanism | Let one visit become a rhythm. | Links returning to recurring access |
| Make it personal | Which place would you return to? | Invites a choice rather than a generic feature list |
| Earn the next step | See where it fits in your year. | Offers an editable calendar based on the reader's choices |
| Continue the journey | Begin with a stay at Blyton. | Turns an imagined visit into an invitation to experience |

Weak structure: “Nature heals. Exclusive luxury. AI calculator. Last chance.” Each introduces a different promise, lacks relevant evidence, or asks for a new kind of commitment.

## WhatsApp after a saved calendar

Only use the family, place, month, or link fields if they were actually selected and verified. If no permission exists, this is a draft only.

“Hi {{first_name}}, your year is ready: {{personal_link}}.

You made room for time with your family at {{selected_place}}. You can still change the dates and shape it around your plans.

When you're ready to experience a place, explore a trial stay at Blyton: {{trial_link}}.”

The calendar visit is illustrative. Blyton dates come from live booking availability. Do not imply a selected calendar date is held or available. If the visit is another collective, explicitly distinguish that choice from the Blyton trial.

## Short email continuation

Subject: Your year has room for {{selected_place}}

“Hi {{first_name}},

Here is the year you pictured: {{personal_link}}.

You chose {{verified_motive_phrase}}. Your calendar brings that choice into view, with visits you can move or change.

The next step is to see how a stay feels. Explore Blyton's trial dates here: {{trial_link}}.”

For unknown preferences, say “Here is your saved year” and explain editing. Do not fill gaps with inferred family status, stress, loneliness, or income.

## Generic B2B example

Given verified features: a dispatch tool keeps job assignments and status updates in one shared view.

Thread: **Know where each job stands before the next handover.**

Headline: “A clearer handover starts with a shared job view.”
Body: “See the assignment and its latest status together. Start with one team's real workflow and check whether it answers the questions they currently chase across messages.”
CTA: “See the dispatch demo.”

Do not turn this into “Cut costs by 35%” or “Never lose a job” without product evidence. Demonstration is the next commitment, not a guarantee of ROI.

## Failure cases to test the skill

| Input | Expected decision |
|---|---|
| “Make it a golden thread” on an existing page | Improve the narrative and transitions; preserve approved design and working links |
| A paid trial with no real countdown | State the trial accurately; omit free language and artificial urgency |
| Viewer watched 75% | Use a useful recap; do not declare them solution-aware or imply personal identity from an ad audience |
| Family selected, four people | Personalise around stated together-time; do not assume child ages, school dates, or night-counting rules |
| Old example promises medical cures or investment returns | Learn structure only; never reuse unsupported claims as evidence |
| Missing customer testimonials | Use verified product detail or a demo; do not invent a speaker |
| “Fix this headline” | Deliver a concise edit; do not force an entire strategy workshop |
| Request for copy only | Draft copy; do not generate artwork, configure tools, or send messages |
| A stronger offer is needed | Identify a real improvement in value, effort, or risk for approval; don't fabricate terms |
| Deadline from verified trial-completion rules | State the actual expiry and timezone; do not imply the clock starts on a click |

## Fast review

For each paragraph ask: “What does the reader understand now that they didn't before?” If the answer is nothing, shorten, combine, or remove it.

Read the last sentence of one section beside the next heading. Does the next subject follow? If not, reorder the argument or add the missing explanation. Do not solve a structural gap by adding “Moreover” or repeating the slogan.

Check the page at mobile width. Long headings, a hidden CTA, or a dense paragraph can break a good verbal thread. Verify the rendered destination when implementation is part of the request.
